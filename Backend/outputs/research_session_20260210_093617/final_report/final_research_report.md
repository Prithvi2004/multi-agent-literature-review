- Growing empirical evidence supports blockchain's transformative potential for supply chain traceability, counterfeit reduction, and audit efficiency in multi-party environments.

## Empirical Strength: Counterfeit Reduction
- Strong, consistent evidence across industries: studies demonstrate **42-89% reductions** in counterfeiting in pharmaceutical, luxury goods, and food supply chains [P1, P2, P4, P8, P9].

## Gaps & Variability: Audit Efficiency
- Audit efficiency gains show **mixed results (12-45% improvements)** and significant variability [P7, P9].
- **Coordination challenges** in multi-stakeholder implementations are a primary barrier, with technical solutions alone proving insufficient without proper governance [P10, P11].

## Critical Gaps
- **Standardized measurement approaches** for quantifying outcomes are lacking.
- **Governance frameworks** for multi-party implementations remain underdeveloped.

## Practical Implications
- Blockchain delivers strong anti-counterfeiting results but requires careful attention to **multi-party coordination and governance** to realize audit efficiency gains.
- Integrated solutions combining blockchain with AI and federated architectures represent the current frontier for addressing interoperability and automation challenges.
